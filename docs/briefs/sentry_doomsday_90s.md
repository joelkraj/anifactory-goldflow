## Premise Boundary

Working title: **What If Sentry Mastered His Powers Before Avengers: Doomsday?**

Use Bob/Sentry from the MCU film *Thunderbolts*, including the Void form. Only abilities demonstrated in the movie may form the evidence base; no comic feats, composite versions, assumed immortality, unlimited reality control, or automatic victory over Doom. Complete, deliberate control of those demonstrated abilities is our one changed assumption. Their hypothetical tactical use and all invented Doom events are speculation, not confirmed film events, leaks, or a prediction presented as fact. Do not cancel the premise with an accidental Void takeover.

The audience-facing boundary must appear early in spoken narration and be reinforced visually when moving from film evidence to imagined outcomes. A proposed opening for tone development, not an approved script or synthesized sample:

> Okay, what if Bob actually had this under control before Doomsday? And I mean the Sentry we saw in Thunderbolts—not the comic version. Same abilities, including the Void. But this time, Bob decides what happens. Would that be enough to stop Doom? Let's think this through.

Exact words remain a candidate for later script review. Keep the delivery curious and conversational, with a real question and a small reflective pause; not a trailer announcer, a list of power rankings, or a read-through of the manhwa cadence.

## Argument to Develop

First establish what a reviewed film shot actually demonstrates. Then ask what changes if Bob can choose when and how to use that ability. Include the Void on the same evidence terms: distinguish the visible effect and consequences shown from any proposed new application or uncertain limit. Do not label a speculative use as an already-demonstrated feat.

Give Sentry a meaningful success before exploring Doom's response. A promising tension is strategic rather than a surprise loss of control: a powerful hero still has teammates, competing objectives, and incomplete information. Consider a counterargument, then a qualified conclusion. The previously discussed Doom assault/diversion/team-rescue outline is a story candidate, not a locked outcome; revise it if the film-evidence audit cannot support the necessary abilities or if it reads as certainty instead of theory.

## Presentation

- Exactly 90 seconds in the finished pilot, including pauses, source-audio moments, and the ending; not 90 seconds of speech plus an outro. Fit the approved script/editorial plan before synthesis and verify duration afterward. Do not accelerate Joel or clip off words to force the target.
- Joel's owned clone is the host voice: questions, skepticism, explanation, and audience exploration. Review an opening take before remaining narration under the future guarded pilot route.
- Use inspected 3–5 second movie excerpts as specific evidence and occasional reactions, including Sentry and Void where relevant. Show the actual cited action long enough to understand it.
- Alternate with original host pose/background sections. Framed clips can slide in, pan, settle, or move beside the host when the edit benefits. Plan those movements as ordinary compositing, not generated scene action.
- Gemini/Flow/Codex stills are optional for the original host/background and clearly identified hypothetical concept scenes. Flow video is optional only when a named imagined action materially needs it. No automatic provider assignment, model switch, or generation spend from this brief.
- Preserve optional source audio per excerpt. Default evidence narration stays intelligible; let a selected movie line breathe only in an intentional narration gap. No soundtrack/music or SFX-generation requirement has been selected.
