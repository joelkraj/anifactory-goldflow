My take: a mastered Sentry should be the centerpiece of the Avengers' battle plan in Doomsday. His biggest upgrade wouldn't be more power. It would be reliability.

We're using Bob from Thunderbolts, not the comic-book version. The change is complete control over Sentry and the Void—not a new set of abilities.

The tower fight establishes our starting point. Bob sends Red Guardian through a window and brings him back. He can do more than hit hard; he can move an opponent out of position.

That's the ability I'd build the strategy around. In our version of the fight, Bob drives Doom back and holds his attention while the other Avengers attack the objective. Sending Sentry in alone wastes the advantage of having a team.

The Void adds a second threat. Thunderbolts shows people becoming shadows. Applying that effect selectively is part of our mastery hypothesis, not a feat the film established. And we have no demonstrated basis for calling it an instant win against Doom.

That's where I draw the line: control makes Bob dependable, not automatically unbeatable.

A credible counterplan could force him to defend civilians while Doom pursues another objective. But having Bob simply lose control again dodges the entire premise.

The interesting conflict is whether Doom can outmaneuver a team built around a controlled Sentry. My position is that Bob's greatest contribution wouldn't be replacing the Avengers. It would be giving them an advantage they can actually coordinate around.
