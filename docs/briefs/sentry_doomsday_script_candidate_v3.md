That's where our version of Doomsday begins. We're using Sentry from Thunderbolts, with one change: deliberate control over his movie-shown abilities, including the Void. No comic-book upgrades.

The tower fight gives us the starting point. Bob sends Red Guardian through a window and brings him back. Here, he'd use that same ability to keep Doom away while the Avengers carry the device out.

Instead of charging back in, Doom detonates the escape corridor, dropping the floor beneath the team. Bob breaks off to pull his allies onto the next landing.

Doom follows, only to find Bob turning back in the doorway as the Void.

Thunderbolts shows people becoming shadows, but it doesn't tell us whether Doom could resist the effect. In our version, he won't gamble on it. He takes the longer route.

Bob holds his position instead of advancing. Chasing Doom now would reopen the path his teammates are still using to escape.

Those few seconds are enough. The Avengers get the device clear before Doom can reach them.

That's why control matters more here than another impressive punch. Doom's diversion moves Bob, but it doesn't break the team. Bob saves his allies, returns to the fight, and costs Doom his objective.

Mastery doesn't have to make Sentry unbeatable to make him decisive.
