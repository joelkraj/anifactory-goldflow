Doom wants Sentry to use the Void. That's the trap.

Here's our Doomsday scenario: Bob has mastered his movie-shown powers, including the Void. No comic upgrades.

Civilians are boarding the last evacuation transport. Bob throws Doom clear of the loading ramp.

Thunderbolts gives us that move: he sends Red Guardian through a window, then pulls him back.

Doom changes targets. He blasts the ramp, leaving one civilian hanging over the drop.

Bob starts pulling them back. Doom advances while he's occupied.

That's the opening Doom wants: a rescue taking time, an enemy coming back, pressure to end the fight in one move.

But look at what the Void actually does. People become shadows. Controlling when to unleash it doesn't establish that its effect would spare the people beside you.

Bob refuses the bait.

Yelena takes the civilian's hand. The team holds Doom back until Bob finishes the rescue. Then Sentry turns, throws Doom off the ramp, and the transport leaves.

Doom wanted the rescue to become another Void disaster, with Bob as the threat and himself as the answer.

Instead, the people leave with the team Doom tried to discredit.

That's mastery: Doom can force Bob to change tactics. He doesn't get to choose what Bob becomes.
