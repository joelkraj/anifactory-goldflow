## The proposed change

Keep the masked host, room set, white-edged character cutouts, moving evidence cards, natural authoritative Joel delivery, and no burned-in captions. Change the encounter, not merely the decoration:

- Concrete stakes: one endangered civilian and the last evacuation transport replace an unexplained device.
- Specific counterplay: in this invented encounter, Doom wants Bob's escalation to discredit the rescuers. This is an authored motive, not a verified MCU Doom characterization or future-film event.
- Expert argument: control over activation does not establish selective immunity for everyone nearby.
- Consequential choice: Bob refuses the Void escalation and finishes the rescue with his team.
- One closing verdict rather than several summaries of the same win.

**Direction change needing approval:** Void is central as film evidence and Doom's desired escalation, but Bob does not activate it during this invented rescue. This is not V3's controlled-Void doorway deterrent. If the operator wants successful offensive Void deployment in the hypothetical encounter, this candidate should be reconsidered rather than quietly treating that requirement as fulfilled.

An intermediate activation-and-reversal version was rejected during independent challenge: civilians were already nearby, and Bob's Sentry attack already worked. Adding an empty approach did not establish a safe effect radius or a reason to escalate. Do not restore that sequence through animation.

## Picture-first beat plan

This is an editorial plan, not locked timecodes or a shot-count quota. Each change must reveal new information; cropping the same portrait again does not count as visual variety.

| Beat | Picture and visual job | Sound and timing intent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook: Doom wants Void | Original host's assured pose, then distinct Doom/actual Void evidence imagery as a clearly identified proposition; do not animate Bob transforming at the evacuation. | Clean voice entry; one restrained accent if it helps the reveal. |
| Movie-only premise | Brief room-set host return; recognizable human Bob image. | Natural statement, not an apology or trailer voice. |
| Establish the rescue | One wide authored view fixes transport, loading ramp, civilian and Bob/Doom positions; follow with the endangered person's perspective. | Establish the environment quietly. No sustained diagram explanation. |
| Bob sends Doom clear | A meaningful action pose/receiver view, then the genuine window excerpt. | Evidence card enters before the decisive action and settles. Reserve the complete five-second excerpt as a possible original-audio window. |
| Doom changes targets | New Doom decision angle, partial ramp damage, civilian hand at the broken edge. Leave usable access for boarding and the later confrontation; do not show total collapse. Maintain the established geography. | One motivated break/impact cue; no unrelated movie explosion presented as this event. |
| Rescue versus advance | Alternate the civilian, Bob's rescue action and Doom closing distance. Show time pressure without an invented countdown. | Clear action rhythm, not a whoosh at every cut. |
| Why Void is the trap | Host marks the analytical turn; actual five-second shadowing evidence is unobstructed. | A second possible source-audio window. Narration and score leave room for the evidence. |
| Bob refuses escalation | Return to unchanged Sentry, with the civilian/teammates still legible. A Void image used as a considered alternative stays in the host/evidence format, never as an event occurring in the rescue. | A brief held decision; avoid a transformation SFX implying activation. |
| Shared rescue and counterattack | Yelena receives the same civilian; teammates buy time; Bob turns back. Action, affected person and result get different useful views. | Resume momentum; one selected hit rather than repeated generic swishes. |
| Payoff | The passengers leave with the team while Bob stays. Show the actual mission result before the host's final judgment. | Let departure breathe, then one verdict; no second outro thesis or dead tail. |

Keep the room and plain-background formats recognizable, but vary the subject matter: person, relationship, location, action, reaction, consequence. The host returns at genuine argument turns rather than occupying every frame. White borders and easing are a house treatment, not the substance of the edit.

Maintain the established 3–5-second limit for each actual movie excerpt. Do not loop evidence to fill missing story time. Original audio remains optional per reviewed clip. SFX and music candidates require their own usable provenance; this plan authorizes neither acquisition nor synthesis.

## Timing and acceptance

The delivery target remains exactly 90 seconds. This 207-word draft is unvoiced and has **no measured runtime**.

A working budget is about 70 seconds of natural narration, two five-second evidence windows, and roughly ten seconds of motivated action, decisions and breathing space. This is planning arithmetic, not measured fit or permission to pad. The actual approved Joel take determines the edit. Do not accelerate the voice, clip words, repeat footage, stretch cards or silently rewrite approved text to force 90 seconds. If a natural take and intentional action cannot fit, return for a scoped text/timing decision before producing the full take.

1. Does the opening promise a specific argument, and does the final action deliver it?

4. Do different images supply people/place/consequence rather than disguise repeated portrait reuse?
5. Do motion and sound emphasize narrative changes while leaving the film evidence readable and Joel intelligible?
6. Does the ending show the rescue and deliver one fresh judgment without repeating itself?
