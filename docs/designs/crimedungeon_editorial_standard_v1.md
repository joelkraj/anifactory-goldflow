# CrimeDungeon editorial standard

For recurring execution, the [house style and required editorial process](crimedungeon_style_bible_v1.md) now supplies concrete treatments, package/beat review and stale-review checks. This document retains the foundational objectives and comparison criteria.

Working creative direction accepted by the user, September 9, 2026. Applies to research, case selection, packaging proposals and editorial review. This is not a registered production route or a script/media/upload approval.

## Objective and order of work

Build the best original documentary that a real case and its obtainable evidence can support. Aim to surpass the references in premise, package, hook, pacing, rehooks, segmentation and conclusion. Duration emerges from the story and useful material: ten minutes, twenty minutes or hours are all possible. There is no discovery gate based on raw recording hours or a predetermined finished runtime.

Begin with a compelling viewer premise and test it against the recordings and factual record. Develop the title and thumbnail concept early; confirm that the film can deliver their promise before committing to the full script. Select and log source scenes, construct the investigative progression, then write narration that connects and interprets those scenes. Revisit a premise if source inspection cannot support it. A long recording alone does not make a strong video; a saturated case needs a distinct worthwhile treatment.

## Packaging

- **Premise:** a specific situation, contradiction, discovery or changing account that viewers understand without already knowing the names. Identify the question, its payoff and what our treatment adds.
- **Title:** promise a particular investigative story supported by evidence. Draft alternatives with different promises rather than interchangeable sensational adjectives. Assess the title, thumbnail and opening together.
- **Thumbnail:** default to a compelling frame from the actual case footage—a meaningful interaction, person, object or discovery—with little or no wording. Crop and contrast should create a clear focal point at small size. Use minimal text/overlays when they help. A bodycam treatment may retain authentic camera information, but must not invent timestamps, recording provenance, expressions or incriminating action.
- **Delivery:** the opening begins paying off the package's promise. The pictured moment must exist in the selected material and retain its context. Record exact frames and their use basis when selected; a frame idea is not an acquired asset.

## Review every component

These questions require the actual cut and source context. They are editorial criteria, not automatic approval rules.

| Component | Standard | How to test it |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | Open on a compelling recorded moment that establishes the central question and enough context to understand it. | Can a fresh viewer explain the situation, stakes and reason to continue? Does it deliver what the package promised? |
| Pacing | Preserve consequential action, useful dialogue and meaningful pauses; remove redundant setup, recaps and inert passages. | What is lost if a passage is cut? Does a longer exchange earn its time? No universal cut-every-N-seconds rule. |
| Rehooks | A discovery, contradiction, changed objective or incomplete answer creates the next question. Resolve promises along the way. | Identify the evidence motivating each question and the payoff location. Does interest survive removal of ominous narration and music? |
| Segmentation | Each segment has a question, source scene, development and consequence. Boundaries follow changes in the investigation. | What changes in our knowledge, the competing accounts or the next action? Are chronology/location resets clear? |
| Visuals/transitions | Footage carries the principal scenes. Cards, maps, documents, text and cutouts clarify particular information. | Why does each presentation change help? Inspect entrances and exits in playback; screenshots cannot establish transition timing or dominant visual mode. |
| Sound | Original dialogue and location sound carry narrative value. Narration adds context and yields when the recording can carry the moment. | Review timed handoffs and intelligibility. Distinguish original, editorial and recreated sound. Automated reports/waveforms are not a listening decision. |
| Retention design | Give viewers an understandable reason to continue and substantive payoffs throughout. Reduce confusion, repetition and stalled questions. | Use fresh-viewer attention/comprehension feedback before release and actual audience data afterward. Views do not disclose private reference retention. |
| Conclusion | Resolve the central promise, explain what the evidence established, and provide the verified outcome and human consequence. | Does the ending answer the opening question? Are findings, allegations and remaining uncertainties distinguished? Does the closing overstay its purpose? |

## Comparison and measurement

The [segment picture and sound specification](crimedungeon_segment_style_v1.md) adds concrete treatments for original footage, audio/transcript scenes, framed footage, documents, geography, chapters, cutout recreations and conclusions. Review text timing, visual hierarchy, texture, movement, sound and entrances/exits as part of each scene—not only story structure.

Use the strongest relevant reference passage for each component. Record its timestamp, what it accomplishes, where our cut is stronger/weaker and the next concrete improvement. Watch comparable passages continuously and retain the limits of the observation. Automated watch analysis helps locate passages; confirm important claims directly. Do not adopt estimated footage percentages as measured facts.

Before release, “better” is an editorial judgment supported by specific examples and viewer review. Higher retention or click performance is a separate empirical claim. After release, examine package performance, the first thirty seconds, segment-level dips/spikes, viewing duration and the ending, with appropriate audience, traffic-source and length comparisons. A retention spike can indicate interest or confusion. [YouTube retention guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en) and [CTR interpretation](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154?hl=en). No universal numeric retention or CTR threshold is adopted.

## Next review artifact

Prepare a compact package card for each promising case: viewer premise, proposed titles, an actual-frame thumbnail candidate or labeled frame-to-find, opening source moment, distinct source scenes, central reveal, outcome, provisional scope and source gaps. A tighter premise can make a case with less footage viable. Duplicate recordings do not add unique coverage.

After choosing a package, build a representative footage-led sequence with an original scene, narrator handoff, purposeful explanatory insert and return to footage. Compare the hook, rhythm and clarity before scaling. Preserve the existing Chandler proof as an explanatory-treatment candidate.
