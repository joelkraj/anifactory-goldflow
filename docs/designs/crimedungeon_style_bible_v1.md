# CrimeDungeon house style and editorial process

Version 1 — September 9, 2026. Required for new CrimeDungeon development. This is the recurring editorial standard; dated case plans and historical proofs are applications, not defaults. It supplements factual and production contracts. It does not change frozen episodes or unlock production/publishing.

**The experience: enter a real scene, understand the question, examine the evidence, experience a consequential answer.** Source footage and original voices carry encounters. Narration and graphics explain why the next recording or exhibit matters. The operator must apply this format without Joel having to rediscover the principles on every episode.

## Recurring story structure

1. **Package:** one viewer question and the answer the available record supports. Assess title, actual-frame thumbnail and opening together. Known cases may merit their name; unfamiliar cases may need a relationship/action premise.
2. **Opening:** choose the source moment that activates that question. Start delivering the promise before extended backstory.
3. **Orientation:** give only the people, place, date and stakes needed for the next scene. Distinguish earlier events from testimony or later findings.
4. **Developed scenes:** question → recorded exchange/exhibit → new understanding → consequence. Each answer supplies a reason to examine the next source. Chronology and claim-testing are available structures, selected by the premise.
5. **Payoffs:** close local questions as evidence permits. A promised later reveal must exist in the source plan. Keep explicit links from setup to answer.
6. **Conclusion:** answer the title in plain language, provide the verified current outcome/date, state consequential remaining uncertainty and return to the people affected.

The source pool earns the duration. Ten-minute and hour-long films use the same construction at different depth. Repeated clips, redundant narration and empty holds do not earn runtime. A request for counsel, a demeanor judgment or missing information is not a substitute for incriminating evidence.

### Three opening types

| Opening | Use when | Sequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery or confrontation | A recording contains the premise | Consequential source moment → brief orientation → next question → chronological reset where useful. |
| Disputed clip or account | The package investigates a circulating interpretation | Attributed claim/excerpt → enough orientation to assess it → fuller original exchange/exhibit → concrete clarification and its limits. |
| Outcome with unexplained route | The destination is known; the investigation is the attraction | Actual outcome moment → precise unanswered how/why → earlier source scene that starts answering it. |

TikTok is not compulsory. For a question-led proof, plan for a recognizable question in the first 20–30 seconds and a first substantive payoff around the first minute. These are authored review targets, not universal deadlines or permission to cut necessary context. A longer opening needs a specific reason and useful developments. A 1.5-second source fragment followed by unrelated setup is not automatically a hook.

## Fixed picture vocabulary

At 1920×1080 start with 96px horizontal / 54px vertical safe margins, Arial regular/bold for portable proofs, dialogue at 52px with at most two lines, and essential supporting labels at 32px. Reflow or reposition around faces, real timestamps, publisher marks and evidence. Do not shrink important words merely to preserve a layout. Inspect encoded frames at 360px width as well as full size.

Palette: charcoal `#101317`, off-white `#F2F0EA`, cyan `#74DDE3` for a selected speaker/active item, amber `#E1AD54` for evidence emphasis, muted red `#B75454` for a specific contrast or outcome accent. Write speaker/status in words. Color does not mean guilty, truthful or false. Preserve useful source color and lighting. These are house choices, not sampled reference hex values.

| Mode | Concrete treatment | Entry / exit and purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Source scene** | Recording fills the picture; original aspect ratio and essential scene context survive. Brief speaker ID; phrase captions on restrained dark backing as needed. No permanent giant header. | Direct cut into a meaningful line/action. Hold while understanding or action develops. Reframe for a new focal detail; no repeated decorative punch-ins. |
| **Social claim** | Vertical clip in a large readable card on charcoal; account/date and `ONLINE CLAIM`. Preserve attribution and enough context. | Cut to the primary material that tests it. Give context/correction promptly, avoiding a prolonged accusation montage. |
| **Evidence comparison** | Actual record or two compatible recordings. Establish each, then highlight one row/phrase/time relationship. Preserve a source locator. | A 6–9-frame reveal/reposition is a starting preset when useful. Return to full source when the comparison finishes. No decorative computer interface required. |
| **Map / timeline** | Verified places/times; one current point highlighted. Source inset only when it explains the relationship. Event time and player time stay distinct. | Advance at the relevant speech/source cue. Label genuine chronology changes. Do not animate an unverified route. |
| **Audio / transcript** | Short complete phrases; named speaker; subordinate waveform from the playing stem; dimmed relevant map/exhibit/background. `ORIGINAL AUDIO` or persistent `AI AUDIO RECREATION` with accurate provenance. | Narrator yields. Text enters with speech, not slowly after it. Clean by default; optional light background grain is an editorial effect, never fake tape provenance. |
| **Reconstruction** | Labeled illustrative cutouts with modest independent entrances/position/scale changes. Supported objects and locations, or clearly illustrative staging. | Use to explain an otherwise unseen relationship/action. End on a supported fact and return to evidence. No invented incriminating gesture/expression. Cast/adapter authorization stays separate. |
| **Chapter / outcome** | Brief question, date or location reset. Ending pairs consequential source with concise dated status where useful. | Direct cuts by default; six-frame text fade/slide is an authored graphic preset. Hold for actual reading needs, with no silent logo bumper or extended repeated recap. |

Source captions are checked dialogue; exact recreated readings have their own disclosure; narrator paraphrases are separate. Prefer an exhibit or non-speaking shot when narration would otherwise cover a visibly speaking witness. If that footage is necessary, make changed audio authorship clear.

## Sound and pacing

Narrator: calm, direct, interested in evidence. Short contextual bridges, then yield. Avoid private-thought assertions, descriptions that merely repeat visible action, and empty promises that everything will change. The scene must remain meaningful with music removed.

Three mix states:

- **Encounter:** intelligible original voice and meaningful room/location sound lead. Silence or a very low bed may be appropriate. Preserve meaningful hesitations; remove inert gaps without making separate moments continuous.
- **Explanation:** narrator leads over restrained instrumental texture. Start auditioning the bed roughly 18–24dB below active speech, then adjust by listening. This is our starting preset, not a reference measurement or sufficient loudness test.
- **Evidence change:** at most one restrained accent for a real reveal/chapter shift; omit it when unnecessary. Reduce/remove score for decisive source words. Never fabricate real-scene police radios, impacts or incidental dialogue.

The current private mastering starting point is about −16 LUFS integrated with a −1.5 dBTP ceiling, subject to the locked adapter. These are technical targets, not listening approval. Preserve accepted raw narration. Check names, continuity and delivery in the complete mix; do not assume Qwen acting instructions or numerical similarity scores establish naturalness.

Use direct picture cuts by default. A short sound carry can connect shots only while preserving speaker and recording-time clarity. No mandatory cut-every-N-seconds rule, constant riser, highlight click or silence added to reach a runtime.

## How to improve on the references

| Component | Specific improvement target | Evidence required to claim it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Package / hook | A clear question, immediately made tangible by the opening | Reviewer unfamiliar with the edit accurately states the promise after seeing the package/opening. |
| Pacing | Each passage develops action, understanding, uncertainty or human stakes | Timestamp what each beat adds; identify repetition and justified pauses in continuous playback. |
| Rehooks | New evidence or a partial answer creates the next question | Each setup names its later source-backed payoff. |
| Segmentation | Viewer understands what changed and why the next source matters | Question/evidence/consequence line for each segment plus independent comprehension review. |
| Picture / sound | Decisive detail is readable; source immersion and speaker identity remain clear | Compare actual sequences, phone readability and complete sound handoffs. |
| Factual clarity | Source, observation time, claim and inference are distinguishable | Full-context review, readable underlying evidence, stated limits. |
| Conclusion | Original promise resolves with a verified dated outcome | Closing answers the package; key facts do not rely solely on viral claims. |

Before release, use `behind`, `matches`, `stronger on [specific criterion]` or `not reviewed`, with candidate/reference timecodes and reasons. An average score cannot cancel a broken promise, missing context or unintelligible line. Better retention/CTR remains a post-release measurement.

## Required recurring process

Keep one compact development packet using the [packet contract](../workflows/crime_editorial_review_packet.md) and [review worksheet](../workflows/crimedungeon_editorial_review_template.md). Research and internal revisions are autonomous. Joel is not the routine source logger, fact-checker or assembly editor.

1. Establish the package promise and selected scenes before writing narrator bridges.
2. Log exact original-player windows and necessary before/after context. Track audio and picture origin separately. Untimed captions are leads, not final edit points.
3. Draft beats with incoming question, source/exhibit, knowledge change, picture/sound treatment and payoff link. A complete film resolves its main promise; a proof states its bounded local test.
4. Obtain a separate editorial pass on package alignment, source context, payoffs, media motivation and factual limits. Revise internally. Unreviewed is not a pass.
5. Before new candidate narration/composition run:
   `node scripts/crime-editorial-review.mjs --packet /absolute/editorial-packet.json --format markdown`.
   The read-only tool checks structure and declared dispositions; it cannot establish their truth. Retain exact packet version and result as development evidence. Bind the reviewed packet as an additional stage input where supported by the future selected identity.
6. Build only through the applicable guarded workflow. This preparation check is a required operator step, **not wired into historical controllers** and not script, factual, media, listening or release approval.
7. Review the exact encoded cut continuously for picture and sound, recording actual coverage. Frame sheets, ASR and technical checks supplement playback. If full listening is unavailable, deliver an explicitly marked review candidate and do not claim format approval.
8. When premise/title/source scope changes, recheck applicability. Previously rendered footage can remain useful without remaining the opening. Review the changed packet again; previous passes are not inherited.

Do not present a candidate as a solved format when it misses the package, lacks a demonstrated payoff, relies on unlogged essential sources or has unreviewed listening claims. Report the narrow outstanding issue and continue independent authorized work. Existing identity, spend, factual and publication requirements remain.
