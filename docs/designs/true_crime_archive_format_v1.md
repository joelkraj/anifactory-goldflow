## Editorial promise

The viewer follows an investigation as recorded accounts meet evidence. Bodycam, interviews, calls, surveillance and testimony carry consequential moments. Narration supplies orientation, explains what a record establishes, and moves the viewer into the next scene.

Choose an investigation with an understandable central account, several consequential recorded exchanges, evidence that changes the account, and a verifiable legal outcome or clearly stated unresolved status. Before committing runtime, locate enough usable source material to show this progression. A compelling written case with little relevant footage needs a different treatment or further acquisition.

The film should leave the viewer able to explain what happened, how the investigation established it, and which material uncertainties remain. Distinguish victims as people through sourced, proportionate context. Preserve the difference between a dramatic account and a supported conclusion.

## Opening and scene construction

The three reference samples reset to the main chronology around 1:16–1:40. A **60–100-second opening** is our initial working range, not a timing gate or universal channel rule.

| Opening element | Working direction |
|---|---|
| First recorded moment | Approximately 5–15 seconds of a consequential encounter or answer, retaining enough context to understand who speaks. If a later moment is used, date it. |
| Orientation | Brief narration names the people, situation and question the viewer needs now. Establish the specific promise made by the title. |
| Supporting source beat | A second relevant exchange or the immediate continuation. Tease the investigative problem without inventing an internal realization. |
| Reset | Visibly mark the earlier date/location and begin the main investigation. A teaser need not reveal every later evidentiary finding. |

After the opening, build scenes around **recorded interaction → necessary explanation → evidence or next action → another recorded interaction**. Not every scene needs every component. The source may itself explain what changed, making an additional narrator paraphrase unnecessary.

Start with the question the viewer needs answered. Retain the answer and consequential follow-up. Let an evidence item answer, limit or complicate that exchange. End by making the next investigative step intelligible. Avoid an arbitrary countdown of clues if the actual sequence is stronger as unfolding encounters.

Source passages in the samples often last roughly **45–120 seconds**, with useful exchanges lasting two or three minutes. Narrator bridges are often brief, but the checked samples also contain minute-long explanation and longer narrated setup. These are approximate local observations, not measured shot lengths. Do not force cuts every few seconds or impose an invented whole-film source/narrator percentage.

A paper edit must budget retained source dialogue, new narration, purposeful nonverbal time and transitions separately. A logged two-minute window is not two minutes of accepted screen time. Count repeated cold-open footage once when measuring unique source coverage. Reduce runtime or expand the source pool when the story budget depends on retaining every candidate.

## Narration and dramatic emphasis

Use a composed, specific voice. Name the time, action and evidentiary consequence in plain language. A bridge can do one of four useful jobs: orient the viewer; clarify a record; qualify an inference; introduce the next action. Connect scenes through what investigators did or what a source shows.

Keep allegation, attributed account, expert testimony and court finding distinct. A witness's answer is evidence of what they said, not automatic proof of the underlying event. A prosecutor's motive argument remains attributed unless independently established. Later evidence cannot silently become the knowledge of an officer in an earlier interview.

Anticipation can arise from an unresolved question or a documented contradiction. Avoid asserting a subject's thoughts from their face, pauses or posture. A counsel request is not a confession or evidence of guilt. Do not construct a reaction using a different point in the recording. Mark material jumps between nonadjacent exchanges and preserve qualifications that change their meaning.

Do not imitate the reference narrator's identity, borrow its scripted prose or assert its claimed research effort as ours. Draft narration from our evidence packet and exact selected source exchanges. Read/listen to the resulting cut before deciding final performance and pace.

## Picture, graphics and captions

The checked frames support a vocabulary of source recordings, date/location maps, timelines, highlighted records, audio waveforms and outcome cards. Use these when they communicate information, with our own consistent visual design.

| Picture type | Working treatment and provenance |
|---|---|
| Interview/bodycam | Preserve enough environment and speaker context to understand the interaction. Crop for legibility without changing meaning, hiding necessary context or substituting a reaction. Retain relevant native timestamps/redactions. |
| Date/location card | Clear date and place at resets or jumps. Separate event date from recording/publication date. A map shows only the location precision supported by its source. |
| Document/exhibit | Present the actual relevant excerpt and one focused highlight, with source/date reference. Authored explanatory graphics must be identifiable as our presentation. A fake database interface must not masquerade as an official record. |
| Timeline/comparison | Show one consequential relationship at a time. Distinguish a claimed itinerary from a verified movement, and devices from people. |
| Audio-only source | A restrained waveform, speaker identification and legible dialogue can carry genuine recorded audio. Waveform appearance does not authenticate a recording. Verify its origin first. |
| Reconstruction | Explicitly identify recreated picture/audio and its factual basis. Keep it separate from archive provenance and source-footage totals. Never synthesize a missing evidentiary quotation. |
| Ending | Dated legal outcome, followed by any material later change. Keep text within space that remains readable with an end screen. |

Large one- or two-line dialogue captions with dark backing appear in the samples; white, cyan and pink/red treatments vary. For our edit, use readable phrase-level captions with consistent speaker identification. Color may aid distinction but must not be the only indicator. Verify words against audio; auto-captions only locate passages. Avoid filling the picture with both dense narration text and full dialogue captions.

A sampled Attic call is visibly marked **AUDIO RECREATION**. Its provider analysis misclassified it as raw 911 audio. This is why every passage needs origin verification even when it looks like a documentary recording. In Halderson specifically, the initial report was in person: a recreated 911 call would be a fabricated event.

## Sound direction

The automated reference analyses describe a low, measured male narrator, dark atmospheric music and retained location sound. These are **provider impressions**, not independently measured mix specifications or end-to-end listening findings.

Our working direction is restrained tension, intelligible source dialogue and an original narrator performance. Keep location/room sound where it sustains the scene; control music under speech and remove it where it obscures or overstates the evidence. Effects should not suggest an event occurred in a source recording when it did not. Do not adopt specific LUFS, EQ, narrator speed or effect frequency as measured properties of Dr Insanity from this study.

Source-audio review must check speaker identity, transcription, abrupt edits, masking, clipping and intelligibility across the actual selected passage and context. An eventual whole-cut listening review resolves the mix; a caption check does not satisfy it.

## Packaging

The current 12-thumbnail sample has **11 with no added headline**, one with two words, and none with evidence insets or panel collages. All use recorder-style decoration. The strongest recurring visual is a readable person/action in a scene; several faces are small. Titles use role, relationship or event rather than full case names and run 7–10 words in this sample. These observations predict no CTR outcome. [Detailed packaging study](../../research/dr-insanity-format-2026-09-08/packaging-study.md).

Our packaging should make one specific, supported promise and pair it with a clear, contextual source image. Avoid adding arrows, collages, exaggerated expressions or imitation channel/recorder branding simply to resemble the reference.

For Halderson, the user's accepted title **His Parents Never Made It to the Cabin** and thumbnail concept **A FAKE / TRIP**, with yellow FAKE and a real interview close-up at right, remain the working package. The three-word yellow treatment is our variant, not the reference's dominant current style. A text-free version of the same appropriate source frame is a possible later comparison, not an approved replacement or approved test. Final source frame and rendered thumbnail remain to be reviewed.

## Development artifacts and pipeline requirements

Research can proceed through source discovery, evidence review, a paper edit and exact acquisition drafts. Keep those documents separate from production artifacts. This design proposes the following future capabilities; it does not claim they already exist:

1. **Source inventory and acquisition state:** publisher/custodian, original versus derivative, exact edition, access/use basis, file hash when acquired, tracks, completeness, redactions and delivery history.
2. **Three-clock clip mapping:** event time, exact-master source time and output time, including upload-to-master reconciliation, selected dialogue plus context, speaker identity and listening evidence.
3. **Claim-to-edit mapping:** each material narration claim, caption and evidence graphic linked to a reviewed source and scoped conclusion. An automated structural validator cannot establish factual truth.
4. **Mixed audio and picture assembly:** substantial contextual source passages with their audio, separately approved narration, source captions, maps/exhibits and any labeled reconstruction. The private footage tool's 3–5-second library clips cannot supply this episode workflow.
5. **Review and release chain:** source-context and factual review, exact script/media approvals, render and whole-cut audio QA, profile-appropriate package approval and the existing private-first/publishing gates.

Implementation needs an explicit applicable true-crime editorial contract and a supported media workflow before any production preflight or provider/media work. Do not substitute the Asset Afterlife, manhwa or bounded avatar identities. The existing [documentary guidance](../pipelines/documentary.md) is useful for evidence separation; its object-led creative rules and generated-visuals route are not this format. The [private footage contract](../workflows/footage_clipping_workflow.md) remains library-only.

## Review questions for the first cut

- Does the first minute deliver the title's premise with a real, contextual interaction?
- Can the viewer follow who is speaking, the date, and what was known at each point?
- Does every retained source exchange develop the case, and every narrator bridge add needed information?
- Are claim, contradiction, qualification and outcome clear without unsupported mind-reading or misleading juxtaposition?
- Are every quotation, caption, source frame and document reference tied to the exact reviewed input?
- Does the film remain engaging at the length earned by its material, with readable graphics and intelligible original speech?
