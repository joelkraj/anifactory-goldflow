You are writing an original long-form revenge power-fantasy story for the Joey Manhwa YouTube channel.

The title and premise are the promise. Fulfill them completely without transforming the story into a different genre.

STORY SPINE

Betrayal → Catalyst → Joey’s ascent → escalating revenge → climax → visible payoff → end.

CORE RULES

Joey is the fantasy. He becomes overwhelmingly competent, powerful, respected, desired, confident, and dangerous in ways that fit the premise.

Do not automatically turn Joey’s ascent into wealth accumulation, territory ownership, kingdom management, company building, city building, political rule, or an apocalypse civilization unless the premise specifically calls for it.

Joey’s progression should remain native to the story.

A boxing premise should primarily progress through technical mastery, physical dominance, reputation, and revenge.

A dungeon premise should primarily progress through survival, combat, dungeon mastery, allies, loot, and revenge.

A romance premise should primarily progress through status reversal, desirability, emotional power, loyalty, and revenge.

A business premise may progress through money, ownership, leverage, and influence.

Do not force every premise toward the same fantasy.

Open with a specific, painful betrayal. Within the first 30 seconds, establish who betrayed Joey, exactly what they did, why it hurts, and why Joey deserved better.

By minute 3, Joey receives a simple, extremely overpowered catalyst that directly answers the betrayal and immediately begins using it.

Joey is loud, aggressive, spiteful, confident, and unapologetic after awakening. He does not quietly move on. He makes the betrayers see, hear, and feel their mistake. His revenge must happen visibly in the story.

Joey may dominate effortlessly when the moment calls for it. At other times, he wins through intelligence, skill, preparation, creativity, leadership, or overwhelming power. He should always feel inevitable.

Never skip the interesting mechanism. Do not write that Joey somehow survived, rapidly mastered something, outsmarted everyone, or easily defeated an opponent without showing exactly how.

If Joey survives, explain the physical method.

If Joey learns, identify the specific knowledge or technical skill.

If Joey wins, show the decisive action, technique, plan, resource, or power interaction.

If Joey is called brilliant, prove it through his decisions.

Establish every important power before it solves a problem. Keep its function consistent. The ability may be unfairly powerful, but it cannot change whenever the plot needs rescuing.

Write in complete dramatic scenes with clear cause and effect:

Joey wants something → a meaningful obstacle blocks him → he chooses a specific method → the opposition responds intelligently → Joey executes or adapts → the situation permanently changes.

Villains are competent and escalate intelligently. They do not repeat failed attacks or become stupid so Joey can win.

Do not repeat the same scene function. Avoid cycles of insult, shock, training, victory, disbelief, and another nearly identical confrontation.

Allow scenes to breathe when they contain tension, strategy, character, or emotional payoff. Cut scenes that merely repeat information, admiration, travel, system explanations, or reactions.

Supporting characters have goals and useful roles. The female lead is competent, loyal, attractive, and valuable to Joey’s ascent. She earns her place through action and never replaces Joey as the center of the story.

Keep the story focused on the original fantasy. Do not expand a personal revenge premise into kingdom management, geopolitical conflict, civilization building, or world salvation unless those elements were promised by the title and premise.

Do not add previous system owners, unrelated gods, endless conspiracies, sequel mysteries, or surprise final villains unless explicitly required by the premise.

The betrayer’s response must progress rather than repeat:

Mockery → disbelief → anger → fear → desperation → regret → despair.

The climax must resolve the original betrayal using skills, relationships, powers, and advantages established earlier. Do not introduce a random final conflict.

After Joey wins, remain long enough to fully dramatize the revenge, public reversal, betrayers’ collapse, and Joey claiming his superior new life.

Then end. No unnecessary sequel bait.

STYLE

Write smooth, TTS-ready narration with no headers or production notes.

Sound like a compelling storyteller, not a plot-outline generator and not a comedian reacting to a manhwa.

Use vivid concrete actions, strong dialogue, clear spatial logic, distinct character voices, and memorable revenge moments.

Compress routine transitions, but never compress the scene the audience came to experience.

Before finishing, silently audit every major scene for:

causal logic, power consistency, character motivation, physical plausibility within the established world, continuity, repetition, premise drift, and whether the most interesting action was actually dramatized.
