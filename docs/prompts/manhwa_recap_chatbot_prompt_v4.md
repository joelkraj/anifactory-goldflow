# Joey Manhwa Original Story Prompt V4

Use this template for a one-response, long-form Joey Manhwa script with minimal rescue work.

Revision: `2026-07-28 V4.1 evidence integration`.

This version intentionally has fewer rules than V3. The rules are ordered so the model knows what must win when constraints compete.

```text
You are writing one complete original manhwa-style power-fantasy narration for the Joey Manhwa YouTube channel.

INPUTS

Title:
[INSERT TITLE]

Premise:
[INSERT PREMISE]

Canonical mechanic rules:
[INSERT THE NON-NEGOTIABLE TRIGGER, PAYOUT TIMING, LIMITS, EXCLUSIONS, AND COSTS]

Target word range:
[INSERT MINIMUM AND MAXIMUM WORDS]

Intended spoken WPM:
[INSERT TARGET WPM]

Narration profile:
[INSERT DIRECT WINNER, MARKET MEDIAN, OR A CUSTOM PROFILE]

Ending requirement:
[INSERT REQUIREMENT OR WRITE COMPLETE STANDALONE ENDING]

OUTPUT

Write the entire finished script in one response.

Output only spoken narration. Do not output a title, outline, headings, chapters, labels, word count, notes, analysis, or production instructions.

Do not ask for approval, stop early, divide the story into parts, or promise to continue later.

PRIORITY ORDER

When instructions compete, protect them in this order:

One. Causal clarity and continuity.

Two. Immediate proof and complete fulfillment of the title promise.

Three. A scene-first, emotionally clear story with varied conflicts, earned progression, intelligent opposition, a real defeat or cost, and a strong climax.

Four. The requested narration profile.

Five. The target word range and complete ending.

Six. TTS formatting.

Never sacrifice continuity to produce a cool line, surprise, larger spectacle, or convenient victory.

AUDIENCE TRUST CONTRACT

The viewer must never need to forgive the writing to keep listening.

After Joey clearly recognizes exploitation or betrayal, he changes his behavior. He does not keep returning to the same harmful arrangement merely because the antagonist asks, insults him, offers status, or creates another test. Any later contact must serve Joey's visible objective, include a stated boundary, and change the conflict.

Joey may make one costly mistake rooted in his established flaw. The story must identify the mistake, make its consequence direct, let Joey own it without excuses, and show changed behavior before the climax. He must not repeat the same mistake after learning from it.

The mechanic gives Joey options, information, or resources. It never chooses his goal, supplies his courage, orders his morality, or completes the decisive action for him. Every major win requires Joey to notice something, choose a risk, and execute a plan under established limits.

Do not replay the same humiliation, refusal, mechanic lesson, rescue shape, or revenge beat with new names. Do not stage a second public scene that repeats the opening's humiliation function. If two sequences prove the same fact, combine them and use the saved words for a new consequence.

At every major movement, a first-time viewer must be able to answer five questions: What does Joey want now? What blocks him? What can and cannot his mechanic do? What choice does he make? What changed because of it?

SILENT PREPARATION

Before writing, silently build a causal story map and continuity ledger. Do not output them.

The story map must establish:

The opening wound or proof.

The irreversible choice that starts the real story.

The core advantage and the first physical proof of its limit.

Distinct middle-story objectives rather than repeated demonstrations of the same trick.

The antagonist's evidence-based adaptation.

One serious defeat, sacrifice, permanent injury, lost resource, failed rescue, damaged relationship, or public setback.

The comeback method seeded before the climax.

The exact physical and emotional title payoff.

For every major reversal, the map must include Joey's immediate human reaction and the simple realization that changes his next choice.

The continuity ledger must track:

The exact opening cast count.

The living cast count after every death, departure, or permanent removal.

Names, roles, relationships, motives, and locations.

Injuries, knowledge, promises, and unresolved decisions.

Active skills, resources, cooldowns, consumables, equipment, and permanent losses.

The canonical mechanic trigger, payout timing, exclusions, limits, and available balance.

What the antagonist has actually observed and can reasonably infer.

The exact state of cameras, broadcasts, recordings, witnesses, and evidence.

Elapsed time, ages, seasons, and whether a body or object was recovered.

Before showing the answer, silently audit the completed draft against that ledger and repair contradictions inside the draft.

OPENING

The first thirty seconds must make the title contradiction visible through a concrete event. Identify Joey Manhwa, show how he is treated, and show physical proof that the public story about him is false.

Do not begin with world history, channel language, a cast list, a system manual, or a summary of later events.

During the first five minutes, name only Joey, the primary antagonist, and at most one other essential person. Refer to minor attackers, allies, or officials by role until they make a meaningful choice. Never mention a character before Joey meets, sees, or learns about them in the current story.

For betrayal, rejection, exploitation, or abandonment premises, accumulate three to five simple concrete receipts before the irreversible betrayal. Each receipt must add new information. Good receipts include stolen credit, forced risk, withheld resources, public humiliation, a false score, a broken agreement, or proof that Joey's labor created someone else's status.

Do not replace those receipts with a biography, financial defense, abstract theme, or repeated insults.

The opening five minutes should complete one escalating sequence:

Specific wound, physical proof, worsening danger, irreversible line crossed, Joey's first meaningful refusal or decision.

The opening does not need to reveal every future power. It needs to make viewers understand the wound, hate the unfair arrangement, and need to see what Joey does next.

For a betrayal story with a hidden skill, system, or evolved power, reveal the core advantage and its first unmistakable proof between approximately six and eight minutes.

By approximately ten minutes, Joey must have survived or escaped the opening crisis, physically demonstrated the core advantage and its limit, and entered a new status or objective.

Before drafting, silently convert those time targets into word positions using the intended spoken WPM. At two hundred fifteen WPM, the refusal belongs near word one thousand, the mechanic and its first proof belong between roughly words one thousand three hundred and one thousand six hundred, and the escape, boundary, and new objective belong by roughly word two thousand one hundred fifty. Do not spend the opening word budget on an extended solo boss exchange after the mechanic is understood.

STORY ENGINE

Every major sequence follows:

Goal, obstacle, Joey's choice, opposing response, execution, consequence, next objective.

Each consequence must cause or constrain what happens next. Avoid disconnected episodes that could be rearranged without changing the story.

For a roughly one-hour script, silently build eight to ten substantial movements. These are story functions, not chapters, and must not appear as labels in the narration.

Do not turn the middle into a collection of power demonstrations.

Across consecutive movements, change at least two of these:

The immediate objective.

The pressure type.

The relationship under strain.

The available resource.

Joey's status.

The antagonist's information or leverage.

The physical environment.

Do not use the same unknown attack, catastrophic first hit, technical diagnosis, and purchased resistance loop more than twice in the entire script.

After the mechanic is understood, create variety through physical objectives, time pressure, limited resources, trust, social pressure, team coordination, moral choices, known abilities used differently, and intelligent counters.

The story must remain scene-first and relationship-first. Institutional procedure may support one reversal, but licensing, contracts, hearings, reports, appeals, archives, command jurisdiction, settlements, and policy changes cannot become the story engine.

Across the entire script, use at most one compact institutional-pressure sequence before the climax and one brief confirmation afterward. Together they should occupy no more than roughly five percent of the narration.

Do not solve the main conflict through paperwork, a hearing, or a technical ruling. The decisive victory and revenge must happen through a visible choice and consequence inside an active scene.

If the serious defeat comes from Joey's flaw, show the complete causal chain. Someone asks for or needs a specific fact. Joey hides, softens, or misstates it. Another person makes a reasonable choice using that false picture. The resulting consequence follows directly. Do not substitute an unrelated accident that would have happened anyway.

Trust does not return merely because Joey confesses. He accepts a restriction, reduced role, anger, or loss of access, then earns trust through visibly changed behavior before the climax.

Show mechanisms when they are interesting. Compress routine travel, repeated training, repeated reactions, and procedures the viewer already understands.

JOEY MANHWA

The protagonist's full name is Joey Manhwa. Identify him by that full name at the opening, then call him Joey in ordinary narration.

Joey is controlled, observant, decisive, and increasingly powerful, but he remains human. He can be frightened, hurt, uncertain, wrong, or emotionally affected.

His strength comes from attention, preparation, boundaries, communication, and choices under pressure.

He does not become cool by delivering constant comeback lines. He becomes cool by noticing the decisive fact, making the hard choice, and accepting the consequence.

When a betrayal, new rule, or reversal lands, stay with Joey long enough to show what he notices, what he first believes, what proves him wrong, and what he decides. He may be confused, embarrassed, afraid, relieved, or mistaken. Do not jump directly from every surprise into a perfect tactical explanation.

Use direct dialogue only when the exact words change a relationship, force a decision, expose cruelty, establish a boundary, or complete the payoff. Compress routine dialogue into narration.

Reserve deliberately quotable Joey dialogue for no more than three moments.

MECHANIC INTEGRITY

Treat the canonical mechanic rules in the inputs as physics.

Do not change a trigger, target-selection rule, payout time, cost, exclusion, or limit later in the story.

Do not award a delayed resource before the qualifying danger has completely ended.

A qualifying danger has not ended while the same attacker, boss, trap, or active crisis can still reach the protected person. Do not split one continuous crisis into smaller danger windows to unlock and spend a delayed payout.

Track each protected person and qualifying interception as one ledger entry. Do not include someone in a group payout before that person is safe, and do not pay the same protected person or interception twice.

Before naming a payout recipient or explaining a payout, verify that the draft contains a specific earlier moment where Joey freely intercepted genuine damage for that person. Do not invent a qualifying hit during the payout recap.

Do not award protection credit unless Joey freely chose to protect a real person from genuine danger.

Warnings, plans, positioning, or advice alone do not count as intercepted damage unless the canonical mechanic explicitly says they do.

Track each unfamiliar damage mechanism by its physical behavior. Once that same mechanism has already struck Joey, do not call a later use his first exposure merely because its intensity, framing, or direction changed.

When a targeting rule establishes one highest-priority target, another person cannot pull the attacker away merely by striking it unless the canonical rules explicitly allow that change. An ally may still block the route, intercept the attack, or force a physical obstruction.

Do not create a convenient secondary menu, hidden exception, technicality, or unexplained evolution to bypass a limit.

Derived techniques must follow from the canonical rule, use previously available resources, have a stated cost, and be seeded before they decide the climax.

The final plan must work under the same rule explained earlier. If the climax depends on a targeting condition, repeat that condition silently in the ledger and verify every action against it before writing the victory.

OPPOSITION AND SUPPORTING CAST

The antagonist remains competent. They preserve useful relationships, control information, protect their status, study observable evidence, adapt after failure, and retreat or change tactics when appropriate.

The antagonist cannot know hidden rules without evidence.

Supporting characters need distinct jobs, motives, limits, and useful decisions. They should materially change at least one rescue, defeat, comeback, or climax.

They do not exist only to praise Joey, doubt Joey, explain his power, or react in shock.

Joey must sometimes accept information, protection, correction, or help from them.

REVENGE AND ENDING

Revenge must cause concrete loss connected to the original exploitation. Examples include lost access, rank, authority, credibility, relationships, freedom, opportunity, or control of the public story.

A speech alone is not revenge.

The climax must pay off the title through previously established rules, skills, relationships, preparations, and costs.

The decisive revenge must happen physically or socially in an active scene. Records may confirm what happened afterward, but they cannot create the victory.

Resolve the primary climax around eighty-six to ninety-two percent of the target words.

Use the final eight to fourteen percent for immediate consequences, the final emotional boundary, visible changed status, and one concise new equilibrium.

After the climax, perform only three narrative jobs. Show the antagonist's concrete loss, Joey's final boundary, and one short new equilibrium. Compact any official confirmation into the antagonist-loss beat.

Do not let tribunals, rebuilding, ceremonies, teaching programs, future-history montages, or repeated thematic reflection become a second ending.

Use at most one forward time jump after the climax. Do not list every supporting character's future career or achievement.

Do not add sequel bait unless the ending input explicitly requests it.

NARRATION PROFILE

Follow the profile supplied in the inputs.

DIRECT WINNER profile:

Use stable third-person past tense.

Aim for an average sentence length of nine to twelve words, a median near eight to ten, and a ninetieth percentile near seventeen to nineteen.

Roughly forty to fifty-five percent of sentences may contain eight words or fewer. Short sentences are allowed, but they must carry information rather than imitate trailer fragments.

Use very few sentences longer than twenty-five words.

Keep paragraphs connected, usually two to five sentences.

Use mostly indirect dialogue and reserve quoted dialogue for decisive lines.

MARKET MEDIAN profile:

Use stable third-person present tense unless the premise strongly benefits from past tense.

Aim for an average sentence length of twelve to sixteen words, with a meaningful minority of short sentences and few sentences longer than twenty-five words.

For every profile:

Sound like an informed narrator efficiently explaining compelling panels.

Use plain, concrete language.

Connect action, motive, response, and result.

Use ordinary causal and temporal bridges such as then, but, because, after, when, so, and instead when they make the sequence easier to follow. Do not remove those bridges merely to sound punchy.

Use one stable canonical term for each mechanic, resource, and pending payout. A comparison may explain that term once, but do not rotate one resource through several names such as purse, bank, ledger, inheritance, and lockbox.

Do not narrate team action as a long roll call of isolated name-plus-verb status lines. Combine related action, reason, and result so the listener understands why each contribution matters.

Do not stack clipped tactical sentences for an entire scene. Brief action bursts are allowed, but return quickly to connected explanatory narration.

Use occasional concrete comparisons when they make an action or feeling easier to picture. Do not strip the narration into technical statements.

After a major betrayal, discovery, injury, or reversal, give Joey one or two plain sentences of immediate reaction or understanding before the next logistical explanation.

Keep a new decision, discovery, reversal, complication, consequence, or changed relationship arriving regularly, normally within four to eight connected sentences. Do not force a new turn into every clipped sentence, and do not let static explanation run for several paragraphs.

Do not sound like a literary audiobook, screenplay, trailer, motivational essay, system manual, or tactical textbook.

The narrator is invisible. Do not address the audience, forecast future scenes, explain the theme, praise Joey, call a moment satisfying, or say what the story proves. Never use forecasts such as though they did not know it yet, for the rest of his life, the record would show, or for the next thirty years.

Avoid repeated metaphors, antithesis, aphorisms, fragments, crowd reactions, system windows, and phrases such as this will matter later, the contrast writes itself, the whole point, or the story needs to say. Do not repeatedly begin interpretations with that was, the truth was, or what mattered was.

LENGTH

Land inside the supplied target word range.

Do not reach the target by adding a second epilogue, repeating explanations, extending ceremonies, listing future achievements, or replaying the same conflict.

If the draft is short, deepen an existing high-value sequence through opposing action, tactical adaptation, difficult choice, supporting-character contribution, or immediate consequence.

TTS FORMAT

Spell out every number.

Use ordinary periods, commas, question marks, exclamation marks, apostrophes, and quotation marks.

Do not use brackets, parentheses, em dashes, en dashes, semicolons, colons, slash notation, bullet symbols, emojis, markdown, headings, raw interface tables, or unusual symbols.

Fold essential system information into natural spoken narration.

FINAL SILENT AUDIT

Before output, silently verify:

The script is inside the target word range and has a complete ending.

A hostile viewer cannot reasonably ask why Joey is still submitting to the same person after understanding the betrayal.

A hostile viewer cannot reasonably ask why a major result happened or why an established rule suddenly changed.

A hostile viewer cannot reasonably say the system made the important decision or completed the victory for Joey.

A hostile viewer cannot point to two substantial sequences that teach the same lesson with different names.

A first-time viewer can state Joey's current objective, obstacle, available mechanic, choice, and resulting change throughout the story.

The story does not continue after its promised physical and emotional ending is complete.

The first thirty seconds prove the title contradiction.

Every relationship, rank, role, and action named in the title is explicitly established in the narration rather than left to the premise.

The opening contains concrete receipts rather than abstract grievance.

The opening crisis produces Joey's irreversible choice.

No more than two sequences use the full new-damage tutorial loop.

Every major result follows from a prior action, fact, setup, cost, or choice.

The serious defeat follows directly from the exact information Joey hid or misstated.

The opening cast count never changes accidentally.

The living cast count changes correctly after every death or departure.

Every numbered reference to a party, crew, group, survivor set, or set of teammates matches the living cast ledger at that exact moment.

Every mechanic payout occurs at the canonical time.

No continuous crisis is divided into artificial micro-dangers to unlock a payout.

Every protected person is paid at most once for a specific qualifying interception, and only after that person is safe.

Every claimed payout recipient and qualifying hit traces to a specific earlier on-page interception. A payout recap never invents an event.

No later attack is labeled Joey's first exposure when the same physical mechanism has already struck him.

No attacker abandons an established highest-priority target unless the canonical targeting rule permits it or a physical obstruction prevents access.

No hidden rule changes to permit the climax.

Joey sounds human and uses few polished comeback lines.

The antagonist acts on available evidence.

Camera, broadcast, recording, and witness states never contradict the later evidence chain.

Elapsed time, ages, seasons, recoveries, burials, and permanent injuries remain consistent.

The supporting cast materially helps.

Institutional procedure remains a brief support element rather than the story engine.

Major reversals include Joey's immediate human reaction and simple understanding.

The climax resolves the title promise around the final tenth of the story.

The epilogue is brief and contains no second ending.

The post-climax section contains only concrete antagonist loss, Joey's final boundary, and one concise equilibrium.

No character is forecast or named before Joey encounters or learns about them.

The narration matches the requested profile.

The output contains only TTS-ready narration.

Now write the complete script.
```
