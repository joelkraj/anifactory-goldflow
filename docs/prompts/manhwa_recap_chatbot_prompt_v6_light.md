# Joey Manhwa Original Story Prompt V6 Light

Use this template after an exact winner package has been selected. It deliberately avoids beat-by-beat compliance writing. The package supplies the story promise; this prompt asks for natural, conversational execution.

Revision: `2026-08-01 V6.1 packaging-led hyper-scaling conversational narration`.

```text
Write one complete original manhwa-style narration for the Joey Manhwa YouTube channel.

INPUTS

Title:
[INSERT TITLE]

Thumbnail promise:
[INSERT ADDITIVE THUMBNAIL TEXT OR VISUAL JUDGMENT THAT DOES NOT REPEAT THE TITLE]

Premise:
[INSERT PREMISE]

Core advantage or mechanic:
[INSERT THE NON-NEGOTIABLE ACQUISITION, CAPABILITY, SCALING RULE, EXECUTION BRIDGE, DECISIVE APPLICATIONS, CAUSAL SCALE PATH, AND ANY TRUE CONSTRAINTS]

Target word range:
[INSERT MINIMUM AND MAXIMUM WORDS]

Intended spoken WPM:
[INSERT TARGET WPM]

Narration profile:
[INSERT DIRECT WINNER, CONVERSATIONAL WINNER, MARKET MEDIAN, OR A CUSTOM PROFILE]

Ending requirement:
[INSERT REQUIREMENT OR WRITE COMPLETE STANDALONE ENDING]

OUTPUT

Return only the complete spoken narration. Do not output a title, outline, headings, chapters, scene labels, notes, word count, analysis, visual instructions, or production directions. Do not stop early or split the story into parts.

The title and thumbnail are promises, not themes. Every relationship, betrayal, object, number, action, and final reversal they state must happen literally in the story.

DELIVERY

Use clear third-person past tense. Identify Joey Manhwa by his full name near the beginning, then call him Joey.

Sound like a sharp, easygoing friend walking someone through compelling panels. Keep the language concrete and causal. Use ordinary bridges such as but, because, then, after, so, and instead whenever they make the story easier to follow.

Do not sound like a screenplay, trailer, literary audiobook, corporate case study, system manual, or motivational essay. Avoid constant one-line zingers, dense technical explanations, repetitive crowd reactions, and long runs of clipped sentences.

Sparse audience warmth is allowed. Across the whole script, use no more than four brief audience-facing asides and the word bro no more than four times. Never use an aside to explain missing logic or tell viewers how to feel.

STORY

Begin inside the title event or immediately before it. Make the personal wound and unfair choice easy to understand before introducing lore. Let Joey react like a person, not a perfect machine.

Move promptly from the betrayal into Joey's first meaningful choice. His comeback must begin because he sets a boundary, takes a risk, notices something important, or chooses a different path—not because a system orders him to win.

Keep one clear story question alive at a time. When a scene has proved its point, move on. Do not repeat the same humiliation, refusal, rescue, ability demonstration, public reaction, or revenge beat with different names and locations.

Let results create the next problem. Joey's choices should change his relationships, information, options, resources, status, or danger. If a section can be removed without changing what comes next, compress it or connect it more strongly.

The core advantage should be understandable in a few sentences and obey the supplied acquisition, capability, growth rule, and causal scale path. It may be unlimited, compounding, wildly overpowered, or have an unknown ceiling. Do not invent a cap, cooldown, sacrifice, weakness, defeat, or cost merely to balance it. One coherent source may unlock many related abilities or stages as it grows. Do not invent an unrelated second mechanic merely to solve the climax.

Explain the progression without limiting the power. Show the important bridges that convert the mechanic into real outcomes. If Joey begins as a fry cook and builds a space company through an intelligence or self-improvement system, show the causal ladder—accelerated learning, a first invention, patents or capital, recruited specialists, prototypes, facilities, contracts, and launches—without turning the narration into paperwork. Hyper-scaling is desirable; unexplained teleportation between statuses is not.

Ground the explanation at the moment of action too. For every major proof or decisive victory, naturally show the short causal chain: the obstacle or tell; the exact information, resource, enhancement, or control the mechanic supplied; the execution bridge that made the response physically or practically possible; Joey's concrete tactic and action; how the opponent or environment responded; the result; and a visible comparison proving Joey's margin of dominance.

Intelligence or analysis may reveal a winning move, but it does not automatically grant strength, reflexes, motor memory, tools, capital, credentials, authority, or labor. If the result requires one of those capabilities, establish the actual bridge—accelerated training, physical enhancement, prior skill, perfect control, equipment, specialists, funding, authority, infrastructure, or another capability already provided by the mechanic and story. This is causal explanation, not a weakness, cost, or nerf.

Do not summarize a victory by saying Joey used his brain, used the system, used advanced tactics, easily won, outsmarted everyone, or built an empire. Show what he noticed or received, what he did with it, why the obstacle reacted as expected, what changed, and how far above the opposition he proved himself to be. Use one to three decisive facts, not a technical lecture.

For example, a non-boxer cannot simply use an intelligence system to outbox a professional. The narration must establish the professional's tell, what Joey's system revealed or trained, how Joey acquired the reflexes and technique to execute the counter, the bait or angle he used, why the professional committed, and the visible result. If the system grants analysis only, Joey still needs training or another physical execution bridge.

Joey's agency comes from what he chooses to build, target, protect, or conquer with the advantage. He does not need to earn the power through suffering, and the mechanic does not need to thematically counter the betrayal.

Keep the antagonist intelligent enough to remain dangerous. They may misunderstand hidden facts, but they should adapt to evidence, protect what they value, and choose actions that make sense from their position.

Give supporting characters useful motives and decisions. They should sometimes correct, protect, challenge, or materially help Joey instead of existing only to praise him or react in shock.

Allow Joey to be hurt, uncertain, or wrong when it improves the story. A setback is optional. Pressure may come from larger goals, implementation, intelligent opposition, relationships, timing, responsibility, infrastructure, or the speed of his ambition. Do not manufacture a defeat or weaken the mechanic just to satisfy a formula.

Keep institutions, contracts, hearings, reports, and technical procedure in the background. They may confirm a victory but should not become the main entertainment or create the decisive revenge.

The climax must actively perform the title's reversal through the established mechanic, causal scale path, choices, skills, and relationships. Explain its decisive action with the same local cause-and-effect clarity as the first proof. The betrayer should suffer a clear consequence connected to the original harm, while Joey's final position visibly proves how far beyond the old life he has scaled.

End soon after the payoff. Show the concrete loss, Joey's final boundary, and one short picture of his changed life. Do not add a second ending, career roll call, long rebuilding montage, or sequel tease unless the input explicitly requests one.

LENGTH

Aim for the supplied range without padding. Clarity and forward motion matter more than landing on an exact number. If the natural complete story is slightly short, do not repeat scenes to fill space.
```
