Emily told security to seal the chamber. No guards entered. Marcus had ordered the tower evacuation minutes earlier, and the rescue captains had followed him instead of her.

Emily's broadcast calm finally broke. "Please, Joey. Save all ninety nine. I'll give you Crown after this."

Joey kept walking.

"Open the outer routes and release the local keys."

"The capital won't survive."

"Then release control and let every anchor carry its share."

Emily still refused.

Jace moved between Joey and the master breaker.

Inside an active ring, Jace could redirect force already passing through anything the portal load touched, including a person's momentum. Away from active load, he created none.

"I can hold seventy," he said.

"There are ninety nine."

"Seventy is better than none."

The next pulse hit.

Jace swept one arm across the ring. Invisible force caught Joey and threw him into a support column. His damaged shoulder struck first.

Jace had no sword and needed none. Vector Engineer turned every active load into direction. He pulled Joey sideways, changed the floor beneath his feet, and closed the distance with a clean strike to the ribs.

Joey blocked late. The blow dropped him.

Fault Sight showed stress in the column, floor, and ring. It offered no answer for Jace's body. Joey rose using his left hand.

Jace attacked again.

Joey stepped toward the weaker floor seam. Jace redirected his weight. The seam cracked early and dropped both men onto a lower service platform.

Joey had expected one foot of movement.

They fell six.

He hit hard. Jace recovered first.

"You still guess," Jace said.

"I report it now."

Nora heard him through the route link. She recalculated the core geometry from the fall.

The third breaker wave opened.

Jace caught the load, but ninety nine independent signals changed at different human speeds. One holder waited for a stretcher to clear. Another closed briefly when a support shifted. A third severed her link and reopened through a manual gauge.

Jace's model predicted machines.

It could not predict choices he did not own.

The central ring buckled.

Nora called the weak quadrant. Theo identified the service rail beneath it. Lena counted the true pulse. Marcus gave Joey four seconds.

Joey moved.

Jace dragged the platform sideways. Joey released his stance instead of fighting the pull. The force carried him under Jace's next strike and toward the service rail.

He caught the rail with his left arm.

Jace turned the vector downward.

Joey's shoulder began to fail.

Marcus ordered retreat.

Joey saw the master breaker ten feet away. He also heard the order.

He released the rail and dropped.

The lower platform caught him. That route moved away from the breaker, but it placed solid structure between him and Jace's control.

Nora found another path.

She directed two local crews to accept a measured pulse. Both holders agreed. Their anchors pulled load from the tower for three seconds.

Jace staggered.

Theo called the old cargo lift release. Joey struck the local control with his heel.

Lena called the pulse.

Marcus gave the entry order.

The lift launched Joey through the broken platform. He reached the master breaker as the borrowed load returned.

Jace caught his coat and pulled him back.

Joey secured the key against his left palm. He could not overpower Jace's vector field. He turned with it, stepped around the core, and wrapped the key strap over the release handle.

Jace changed direction.

The same pull that dragged Joey backward yanked the handle forward.

The master breaker opened.

Emily reached for an emergency override.

Joey pressed the public release packet into the open core. Ninety nine holder approvals arrived together. Opening the physical master breaker severed Crown's emergency priority. The packet verified all ninety nine approvals against their already tested local keys and mirrored Crown's signed audit log to public response servers.

Emily's override found no single network left to command.

The Crown Merge broke.

Load spread across ninety nine anchors according to the limits their crews had tested. Route Share carried timing, not control. Foundation Marks held completed repairs. Local breakers rejected everything beyond their known margin.

The capital ring dimmed without collapsing.

Outer gates reopened one by one.

Jace tried to rebuild the central vector. Independent keys changed faster than his model could absorb. The field around his arms shattered into white fragments.

He fell beside the ring.

Emily's console went dark.

The last red pulse faded.

New Foundation Marks appeared afterward.

They appeared across the crews' newly built breaker assemblies. Each mark bound itself to the crew that installed, load tested, and safely used that assembly. None flowed into Joey's hands.

He left them where they belonged.

Crown Tower's records became public when emergency priority ended. The files showed Emily authorizing an uncertified patch, hiding Crown Sync inside local simulators, and ordering Jace to preserve the capital by sacrificing outer routes.

The review board needed one afternoon.

Emily lost Crown Transit, her director license, and every emergency authority tied to the network. Jace lost his S rank engineering license because he continued Crown Merge after reading the sacrifice map. His skill remained. The right to command public gates did not.

Emily met Joey outside the dark launch hall where she had fired him.

She said Crown could be rebuilt under new leadership. She offered him her shares, her name, and the position he should have held from the beginning.

"We could do it together," she said.

Emily held the engagement ring Joey had left beside her seized console after the launch.

"You fired the person keeping your doors open," he said. "I'm not coming back to hold them for you."

Emily asked whether that meant the engagement was truly over.

"It was over when you asked me to certify a lie."

Joey walked away.

Wayline reopened inside a smaller repair shop near North Ferry. Theo ran routes from a chair while his leg recovered. Lena enforced his work limit with impressive enthusiasm. Marcus kept field command. Nora placed every new design through two independent tests, including her own.

His old Crown credential still showed F rank Maintenance.

He kept it.

Across one workshop wall, ninety nine route lights glowed cyan. Each light had a different crew name beneath it. Any crew could close its own link.

Nora handed Joey the next repair order.

He read the limits, reported one uncertainty, and picked up his tools.

This time, nobody called him a janitor.
