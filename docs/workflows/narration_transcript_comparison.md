# Narration transcript comparison

ASR comparison preserves Unicode words in NFC form. An accented word remains one word; accents are not stripped and different spellings remain substitutions. The exact apostrophized contraction `I'm` (including a curly apostrophe) compares as `I am`. Unpunctuated `Im`, ambiguous `'s`/`'d` forms, homophones, source text, provider input and recognized text are unchanged.

Numeric comparison uses cardinal groups and descending scales, rather than adding arbitrary adjacent numeric words. Sentence punctuation ends a number. A comma or `and` can join a valid composite such as `one million, two hundred thousand` or `two thousand and twenty-one`; repeated scales remain separate (`forty-two million and eighteen million` is not sixty million). Commas inside digit groups still work, but whitespace does not concatenate separate digit amounts (`210, 200`). Spoken decimals may take a following scale: `two point two million` compares as `2.2 million`. An exact `a hundred`, `a thousand`, `a million`, or `a billion` supplies an implicit one; articles outside those constructions remain words. Ambiguous clock readings, digit-by-digit sequences, and nonstandard number phrases receive no inferred equivalence.

Dollar notation retains its symbol evidence during comparison. Beside an aligned equal amount, `$1` can supply the explicit word in `one dollar`, and `$2.2 million` can match `two point two million dollars`. The currency word must directly follow the amount before punctuation removal; `one. Dollars mattered` cannot lose the following subject just because ASR writes `$1`. A symbol also retains the existing bare-amount equivalence (`a hundred million` versus `$100 million`). Without a matching dollar symbol, `one dollar` versus `one` remains a missing word. ASCII and Unicode minus signs are retained in numeric currency notation. Different values, signs, surrounding omissions, and currency words remain distinct. This changes comparison tokens only; source, provider input, recognized text, and ASR evidence are never rewritten.

`transcript_comparison_version` identifies the derived comparison, separately from the locked ASR/model contract. Provider finalization always rebuilds unit comparisons and delivery decisions from hash-bound raw recognition checkpoints. A comparator revision also rebuilds full-stream comparison and confirmation scope, reusing the exact primary transcription and matching audio/contract/window-bound Medium evidence. Only newly required windows need recognition. Retired confirmation scopes retain their raw evidence separately and cannot contribute old decisions. A comparator update does not authorize new synthesis or satisfy listening requirements.

Before a scoped recovery, preserve the blocked reports and checkpoint in the episode's immutable repair evidence. Use the guarded `tts finalize-provider` command from status to apply the current comparator; do not edit transcripts, source, WAVs or decision JSON to clear a finding. Confirmed omissions, insertions, protected values and endpoints remain subject to the existing delivery and listening gates.
