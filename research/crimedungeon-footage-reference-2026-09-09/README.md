## The correction to our prior approach

The prior Dr Insanity study identified source encounter → narrator context → evidence → another encounter, but turned its proposed implementation into almost continuous narration over records and cutouts. Original scene audio was relegated to an optional later step. Our 64.4-second proof contains 63.38 seconds of retained narrator and no original source-footage/audio scene. Eighteen picture shots improved an explanatory insert; they did not demonstrate the requested documentary format.

The user's clarified direction is footage as the majority presentation, including a footage opening, full-screen encounters, occasional framed footage, supporting text and animated transitions. Case and scene selection need to begin with strong, obtainable recordings, then narration should connect their consequential moments. Existing document/cutout work can serve as short explanatory passages within that structure.

## What to discuss before another proof

Select the strongest moments from this reference together, then specify our opening and representative main scene around actual usable footage. Record each selected reference passage's viewer question, source action/dialogue, narration entry/exit, full-screen/inset choice, graphic purpose, sound layers and transition. The benchmark is a compelling source scene with clear geography, comprehensible dialogue, useful narration and a payoff—not simply more cuts, stronger music or matching colors. Aim to improve clarity, restraint and source traceability while matching its ability to make viewers feel present in the investigation.
