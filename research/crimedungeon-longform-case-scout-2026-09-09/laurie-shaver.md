## Saturation and proposed angle

This is an established, substantially covered case, not a low-competition discovery. vidIQ reported [That Chapter's 28:12 film](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yzDQtyDZT4k) at 1,416,893 views, [Law&Crime/The Crime Atlas's 1:29:09 documentary](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-Q87dQTEDUo) at 2,635,208, and [Annie Elise's 1:34:55 treatment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0lY7Kf1A5Io) at 493,046. These are finished creator works; **zero raw minutes are credited from their runtimes**. This sample indicates substantial existing attention; it is not a complete saturation or performance study.

Possible original editorial question: **What can the recorded testimony and digital trail establish about the claim that Michael had simply left?** A tighter 10–15-minute treatment could open on a consequential courtroom question, establish the disappearance with a short evidence sequence, contrast a limited set of attributed answers with records, and conclude with the actual verdict and sentence. This is a development angle, not a factual finding or frozen script.

Prefer adult testimony and the tested evidence over packaging the child's accusation as an established confession. Do not turn a witness's construction of the concrete slab into a finding that the witness knowingly concealed a body. Broadcaster titles compress distinctions; our exact scene claims must come from the testimony and record.
