## Editorial fit

The attractive narrow progression is **the account at the scene → detectives testing it against the phone recording → the verified court result**, anchored in three or four actual recorded scenes. Graphics would clarify chronology or a precise contradiction; they need not carry the whole episode. The phone evidence involves a dying person's distress, so the editorial question is what brief evidence is necessary to understand the case, with no shock-loop replay.

A longer story would need its own question about the earlier recorded police contacts and how the court evaluated the competing accounts. It should preserve the defense's stated position and the victim's context instead of turning demeanor guesses into evidence. Court TV describes its exhibit presentation as including bodycam, cellphone video and calls relating to the alleged abusive relationship; exact events and claims still need exhibit-by-exhibit checking.

**Saturation is a meaningful disadvantage:** today's metadata lists approximately **6.67 million** views on the 2022 interview upload and **4.20 million** on the 2022 bodycam upload. Numerous reaction and compilation searches reuse them. Those counts establish broad prior exposure, not an age-adjusted forecast for CrimeDungeon. A generic retelling would have weak differentiation.

**Recommendation:** retain Boone in the shortlist as a high-availability candidate, especially for a focused 10–15 minute treatment, but do not choose it solely because hours are available. Next work is a bounded scene log of the bodycam, interrogation and 911 call: exact time windows, story function, intelligibility, duplicate status, source context and use basis. Only that log can justify the final duration.
