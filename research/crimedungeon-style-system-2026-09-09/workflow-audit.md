# Why the editorial standard did not control the proof

Read-only workflow audit, September 9, 2026. Reviewed the repository instructions, editorial standard, hybrid format, segment style, private footage and editorial revision workflows, current audience package, V3 review, and dedicated controller/CLI. No episode, media, code, identity or approval was changed. This audit does not contain a new reference-video viewing claim.

## Finding

The missing element was not another instruction to “use footage.” The existing standard already required a compelling premise, package/opening alignment, source selection before narration, purposeful presentation changes, actual playback review and comparisons to timestamped reference passages. Those requirements remained prose around a controller that verifies provenance, scope, source bindings, complete narration and encoded media. It does not require evidence that the proposed film fulfills its package or that a selected passage earns its duration.

V3 is therefore a successful bounded technical/editorial experiment, not evidence that the recurring format has been solved. Its 60.4 seconds include 46.3 seconds of original audio and 52.4 seconds of one courtroom setup. The source testimony develops a discovery, so a long shot is not automatically wrong. But neither the footage percentage nor removal of narration holds demonstrates a strong opening. The reviewed opening fragment, “really quiet,” is only 1.5 seconds; the cut's relevance depends heavily on its package. It does not present or answer the viral-claim question subsequently chosen for the full film.

The package steering arrived while V3 was in progress. V3 should not be retrospectively described as a failed execution of an identity that never contained that package. The workflow failure is leaving a completed discovery candidate in the role of “our improved proof” after the film's central promise changed, without an explicit applicability review.

## Where the process failed

| Existing requirement | What remained unenforced | Required correction |
|---|---|---|
| Premise and package come first | A title and a source-backed plan can exist without a one-sentence viewer promise or a mapped answer. | Require a package contract and answer location before selecting a production proof. |
| Open on a consequential recording | “Original footage” becomes a proxy for a useful cold open; a short fragment can satisfy the visual rule without establishing the question. | Review the exact opening exchange in context and connect it to the click promise. |
| Narration connects evidence scenes | Correct narration can connect testimony without producing the particular explanation the viewer came for. | Every beat states the change in viewer knowledge and why the next beat follows. |
| Compare against strong reference passages | The comparison can be deferred until after rendering, or based on stills and automated descriptions. | Bind a small set of directly inspected reference passages to the proposed treatment, with observation limits. |
| Picture and sound carry different functions | Footage share can dominate assessment even when a single talking-head composition carries nearly the whole proof. | Record original-audio purpose, picture purpose and explanatory need separately. |
| Whole-cut viewing/listening matters | Technical QA, contact sheets and full-stream ASR are available sooner than a real playback review. | Do not declare pacing, sound handoffs or voice naturalness reviewed without continuous playback/listening evidence. |
| Each proof tests the intended format | A historical or differently packaged cut can remain the working benchmark after the premise changes. | Give each proof one explicit question it tests, and recheck applicability whenever the package changes. |
| Instructions should be reusable | The hybrid design retains an older Halderson “next proof” paragraph beside later Clancy routes. | Keep durable editorial requirements separate from dated case instructions; route to the current style version explicitly. |

The operator should do these checks autonomously within authorized scope. They are not reasons to ask Joel to invent the format or approve every internal research step. A formal script/listening/program approval remains whatever the applicable workflow actually requires.

## Minimal reusable artifact set

Five compact artifacts are enough. They can be sections of one development packet plus the existing machine manifests; avoid creating an administrative document for every scene. Their job is to expose a bad editorial decision before an expensive render.

### 1. Package contract

Record the intended viewer, what the viewer likely knows, the single question, proposed title, actual thumbnail frame or explicitly unacquired frame target, the answer the film can support, the first selected scene, what our treatment adds, and the reference passages being used for comparison. Include case/source cutoff date. For a current social controversy, retain the observed question sample and its method; do not treat the sample as a population poll.

**Do not proceed to a package-specific proof when:**

- The title asserts an admission, contradiction, identity, deception or outcome that has not been checked in its precise wording.
- The opening and conclusion answer different questions.
- The promised answer depends on a source that has not been located and context-checked.
- The thumbnail moment does not exist, implies an unsupported act, or cannot be read at small size.
- The plan offers only a general recap for a specific question-led title.

An evidence-supported change of title or narrower premise is a normal repair. A provocative title is not rejected simply because it is provocative.

### 2. Selected-scene and evidence ledger

For each necessary source: stable URL and publisher, source family, exact original-player in/out, preceding/following context needed for interpretation, exact selected words/action, speaker and date/time being described, picture origin, audio origin/language, linked claim, what the source establishes, what it does not establish, acquisition/reuse basis and availability. Once acquired, bind hashes and local clip positions through the applicable adapter. Distinguish an event clock time from a player timestamp.

**Do not proceed with the selected scene when:**

- Only an untimed transcript, cropped still, reposted allegation or automated summary supports the crucial comparison.
- A missing question, date, speaker, source field or later correction could change its interpretation.
- Separate source moments are represented as one continuous event.
- Reposts are counted as independent corroboration or repeated footage as unique coverage.
- The actual audio language/origin is unknown or differs from the expected source.
- Required evidentiary detail cannot be read or heard in the available asset.

This does not require acquiring an entire recording to study one passage. It requires sufficient context to select that passage honestly and usefully.

### 3. Beat sheet and picture/sound plan

Each row needs: approximate sequence position; incoming viewer question; selected scene/exhibit; new fact or development; narrator's necessary contribution; picture treatment and why; original voice/ambience/score/effect behavior; outgoing question or local answer; source IDs; unresolved risk. The final row answers the package and records the dated outcome/uncertainty. A proof additionally says which recurring edit capability it tests.

**Return for revision when:**

- A beat does not change understanding, action, stakes, competing accounts or the question being investigated.
- A rehook is only ominous language with no impending evidence or subsequent payoff.
- Narration merely repeats immediately audible dialogue without an orienting or interpretive purpose.
- A graphic/card/cutout is selected to create motion but communicates no useful relationship.
- An apparent correction conceals the remaining uncertainty.
- The scope needs a promised scene type that the selected adapter cannot produce.

No universal footage ratio, clip length or cut interval is a pass criterion. A sustained interview answer may earn its time; a rapidly cut sequence may still be repetitive.

### 4. Pre-render editorial review

A second pass, preferably by an independent reviewer, checks the package, ledger and paper edit before new narrator generation/final composition. Record reviewer, exact artifact versions/hashes, source/reference passages actually inspected, each component's `ready`, `revise` or `not reviewed` disposition, and specific fixes. The review may bind as an additional existing stage input; hashing it does not turn its judgment into fact.

Required questions:

1. Does the first source moment make the chosen title's question tangible?
2. Does the opening begin a substantive answer soon enough for this material, or spend its available time on background?
3. Is each narration unit necessary and factual, and does it yield to the source?
4. Is the first explanation demonstrable with the selected exact source windows?
5. Can each rehook name the development that motivates it and where it will pay off?
6. Does the ending fulfill the promise without overstating certainty?
7. What is the strongest plausible critique of this treatment, and has the edit addressed it?

**Do not generate/render the claimed finished proof when** a necessary item is `revise`/`not reviewed`, key source context is missing, the package changed after the review, or the review only says “more engaging” without an edit decision. An explicitly scoped research mockup may still be useful, but must be identified as that rather than a finished format proof.

### 5. Actual-cut review

Bind the exact MP4 hash and package version. Record continuous viewing/listening coverage, device/display size, observed defects by timestamp, comparison passages actually watched/heard, and the disposition of the original test question. Keep technical verification, editorial judgment and user acceptance separate.

Critique the hook, orientation, first payoff, source/narrator handoffs, unnecessary repetitions, meaningful pauses, visual readability, rehooks, segment progression and conclusion. For each concern, state the observed moment, its consequence and the smallest specific repair. Run a fresh-viewer comprehension check where available: what did they think the claim was, what changed, what remains unknown, and what made them want to continue? No leading questions and no need to make the user the routine editor.

**Cannot be called format-ready when:**

- No one has continuously watched/heard the exact delivered cut but pacing/naturalness is described as verified.
- The package question is absent or its answer is delayed by irrelevant setup.
- A source/narrator transition makes authorship or meaning ambiguous.
- A source phrase, name or exhibit is unintelligible at intended viewing size.
- The proof does not demonstrate the capability it claims to test.
- There are unresolved blocking factual issues or an unfulfilled package promise.

“Better than Dr Insanity” requires named, timecoded comparative judgments before release. Better audience retention/CTR requires actual appropriate post-release data; neither technical scores, a second agent's preference nor competitor views can establish it.

## Existing CLI hooks and boundaries

Inspected [dedicated CLI](../../scripts/crime-footage-editorial.mjs) and [controller](../../scripts/lib/crime-footage-editorial-workflow.mjs). They support only `preflight`, `status`, `begin`, `finish`, `fail`; stages are `source_assets`, `narration`, `program_review`. No approval or publishing command exists.

- `validateIdentity` locks explicit sources/windows, use-basis text, exact script/plan references and private scope. It does not validate a package promise or thumbnail.
- `validateCrimeFootagePlan` checks bounded Clancy plan, selected source IDs, claim references, scene origins and ordered complete narration. A claim with a nonempty source-ID list passes the structural source requirement; that check does not prove the claim. There are no fields for viewer question, knowledge change, payoff or reference comparison.
- `beginCrimeFootageProofStage` accepts checked additional `{path,sha256}` input bindings. A completed development packet and review can therefore be retained without modifying a frozen identity or claiming that semantic review has been automated.
- `finish` verifies technical and media-binding contracts and creates immutable candidate receipts. It deliberately keeps approvals false. This boundary is correct and should remain.

Do not retrofit schema fields into a frozen plan: the validator has explicit allowed-key lists and locked hashes. Do not change old controller code in place to impose a new rule on historical identities. For a future supported adapter, a versioned editorial readiness input can require presence, current package/plan hashes and explicit review dispositions. Machine checks can enforce artifact completeness and stale-review rejection; the review still needs actual source judgment and honest coverage.

No new code gate was implemented in this audit. A documentation requirement should not be advertised as a technical enforcement feature.

## Application to the next Clancy proof

The next proof should test whether a viral claim → primary recording → concrete clarification → evidence-motivated next question makes the package work. It needs the actual viral excerpt, the complete relevant Patrick/Hall exchange with observation-time context, and a bounded source-supported answer. The existing discovery cut can supply later orientation; reusing its entire minute simply because it exists would postpone the new promise.

Before adopting “Lindsay Clancy Admitted It. So Why Is TikTok Blaming Patrick?”, identify the exact admission and legal distinction the wording communicates. The title may be viable; the factual predicate must be tested rather than inferred from a defense acknowledgment. The proof must not pretend the Hall/Patrick timing distinction medically explains the difference. The next device question should lead to the actual source-device field, not just a narrator asserting the viral interpretation is wrong.

This is a bounded format test. Passing it would establish that this explanatory sequence works on this selected material; it would not guarantee every future case or prove superior retention.
