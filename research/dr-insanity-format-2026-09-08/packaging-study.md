# Dr Insanity — current packaging sample

Observed September 8, 2026. Research only: no paid tools, media downloads, thumbnail creation, outreach or production changes. Root AGENTS.md read. Sample: the first **12 videos** shown under **Latest** on the live [Dr Insanity Videos tab](https://www.youtube.com/@DrInsanityCrime/videos), spanning the UI's approximate labels from 10 days to 5 months old. Titles were read in the channel UI; every thumbnail was opened individually from YouTube's image CDN and visually inspected in the browser at its displayed **1280 × 720** size. This is one current observation, not a historical/A–B-test audit. Thumbnails and titles can change.

**Main finding:** the current sample relies primarily on a single camera-like scene and a descriptive, dramatic title. Large headline text, arrows, evidence insets and face collages are not the dominant thumbnail pattern. Our approved Halderson concept fits the broader idea of a clear visual and an unresolved story, while making its own choice to use three large words.

## Counted observations

- **11/12** thumbnails have no added editorial headline; **1/12** has a two-word label. Median headline count **0**, range **0–2**. None uses yellow headline type. The one headline is white with a dark outline/shadow treatment; KILLER is much larger than secret.
- **12/12** have visible recorder-style UI: REC with a red dot plus white corner, battery and camera symbols. These are a repeated visual convention, not proof of which camera recorded a scene. Do not infer that these graphics are native bodycam metadata.
- **1/12** has a drawn arrow; **0/12** has an evidence inset, document inset or obvious multi-panel collage. One has a conspicuous channel logo beyond the recorder graphics. A pointing arm or a photographed object was not counted as a graphic arrow/inset.
- **12/12** present people inside a scene. **7/12** depict exterior settings, **5/12** interiors including the vehicle interior. Scale varies substantially: a wide parking-lot view, standing figures, seated attic figure, and tightly framed twins. There is no universal giant-face template. The twin image is the clearest face-dominant composition; several others make gesture/object/action more informative than facial detail.
- Contrast comes largely from the presented lighting, clothing and key objects: white shirt, blue container, pink top, bright vest, flashlight-like illumination and police lights. The sample does not support a single orange/teal grade or a universal black-background portrait treatment.
- The titles contain **7–10 space-separated words**, median **9**. **12/12** identify people by role/relationship or scenario rather than by full case name. **3/12** begin with How; **3/12** use Realizes/Realize. The rest emphasize discovery, encounter or someone leading another person to something. Every title explicitly signals a violent-crime premise; none is framed as a question.

These observations do not establish thumbnail source authenticity, editing history, click-through rate or causal reasons for view counts. No vidIQ scores, paid analysis or performance inference was used. The titles' allegations and wording are being cataloged as packaging, not adopted as factual or editorial standards.

## Applying this to the approved Halderson package

Approved title: **His Parents Never Made It to the Cabin**. Approved thumbnail: a real interview frame on the right; **A FAKE / TRIP** on the left, with **FAKE** yellow. **Keep this approved concept as the working base.**

Consistent with the sample:

- At **8 words**, the title is similarly compact, uses a family relationship rather than a case name, and creates a specific unresolved sequence: a supposed trip whose destination was never reached.
- A genuine interview picture supplies a human subject and a source-recording context. One subject and one short premise avoid a complicated collage.
- The thumbnail and title can complement each other: the title establishes the missing destination, while FAKE identifies the disputed account the episode will test.

Distinct from the sample:

- Three headline words and a yellow emphasis word are our treatment; the sample overwhelmingly uses no headline. The sample's sole headline sits on the right, whereas ours is planned on the left. Neither arrangement is inherently required by the format.
- The approved title does not explicitly say police, killer, murder or body, unlike all 12 sampled titles. That makes our premise less explicit about genre. The real interview context and opening need to establish it promptly; this observation does not justify changing the approved title.
- Our interview portrait must earn its space through a legible, contextual source frame. The current sample often shows an action or telling object in an environment, rather than simply a cropped face. Keep enough of the real interview room/table to read as an interview if the cleared source permits it. Do not manufacture an expression, evidence object or cabin background.

Practical recommendation: retain the two-line text, yellow FAKE and subject placement, but make the image as specific and readable as the source allows. Do not add an arrow, evidence inset, huge logo or imitation REC/battery graphics merely to resemble this channel; the sample does not show a need for more annotation. FAKE must refer to the substantiated trip story, and the first sequence must deliver on that claim.

**Separate, unapproved test idea only:** after obtaining a suitable cleared source frame, a version using that same interview image more fully with no headline would test the text-free tendency observed in 11/12 examples. It is not a replacement for the approved concept, an approved A/B variant or a request to generate a thumbnail now. Actual comparative performance would need measurement; this study predicts none.
