## Visual and sound treatment

Own frame checks establish an alternating visual vocabulary: documentary recordings, dark map/date cards, an explicitly stylized red reconstruction, a timeline beside a speaking witness, a waveform for an audio-only exchange, and a final portrait/outcome graphic. The overlays provide geography, chronology or a readable representation of otherwise limited pictures. At 47:33 the subject and table fill the frame; at 14:35 the witness shares the frame with the timeline. A still sample cannot establish zoom speed, crop changes or cut frequency.

A waveform is a presentation device, not proof of an original recording. Keep authentic source audio, recreated dialogue, narrator speech and unresolved audio origins as separate categories in any later format measurements. The roughly 70-second call above belongs in the unresolved category until its provenance is checked; absence of a label in a few screenshots cannot move it into authenticated source audio.

The 47:33 and 19:04 dialogue text remains visible with YouTube closed captions switched off, supporting that it is part of the editorial presentation. Cyan and pink/red appear in the sampled scenes; this does **not** establish a consistent channel-wide speaker-color rule. The two sampled narration graphics at 01:20 and 48:34 use white display text, with red reserved for the outcome heading. Source-frame branding is visible.

**Provider-only sound assessment:** the watch analysis reports a measured low male narrator, low atmospheric synth/drone, occasional risers/bass accents, and retained location/room sound. Captions include music markers, but these cannot independently confirm score timbre, loudness or mixing. No production audio specification should be derived from this report alone.

## Timing correction and practical inference

The provider reported approximately 35 seconds narrator and 145 seconds source audio in **15:20–18:20**. Native captions contradict that allocation. A rough caption-based partition is **15:20–15:35 narrator (~15 s), 15:35–17:19 source exchange (~104 s), 17:19–18:14 narrator (~55 s), then a short non-speech/music interval (~6 s)**. These total the 180-second sample but are provisional boundaries, not stopwatch listening. Reject the provider's 80.6% source figure; do not substitute this sample for a whole-film ratio.

The transferable editorial pattern is to introduce a question, let a meaningful source exchange develop, then use narration to explain what changed and motivate the next piece of footage. Source blocks can run for minutes when the exchange earns that time; narration can also carry a longer reconstruction/setup passage. This sample supports variable scene-driven pacing, rather than a fixed clip-to-narration percentage. Its assertive judgments and reconstruction detail are stylistic observations, not instructions to invent evidence or infer a subject's thoughts.

The channel's pinned comment reports that a section around 08:30 was removed after publication and points to an extended version. That is a publisher statement, not independently verified platform history. It is a reason to bind these locators to the current upload, and not treat the apparent cut there as a deliberate pacing template.
