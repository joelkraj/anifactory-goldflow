## Recommendation

**Conditional shortlist candidate; strong editorial distinction, weaker immediate police-footage availability.** Public, long courtroom recordings remain playable. Two older links described as the hospital interview and a 911-call bundle are now private. This should be evaluated as a trial/evidence film, with original police recordings an acquisition gap. The central question is how a jury could acquit Danielle Redlick of murder while convicting her of evidence tampering. Her admitted stabbing, initial accounts, self-defense testimony and the separate verdicts offer a coherent story. Avoid a narrator who presumes her guilt of murder or treats an acquittal as proof that the jury accepted every detail of her testimony.

## Verified case position

- Incident: Michael Redlick died following a stabbing in Winter Park, Florida, in January 2019. The [Winter Park Police arrest release](https://cityofwinterpark.org/docs/departments/police/public-information/press-releases/2019/2nd-degree-murder-arrest-danielle-redlick-2019-02-06.pdf) identifies the responding agency and original second-degree-murder/evidence-tampering charges. Its allegations are historical, not the outcome.
- Verdict: June 17, 2022, **not guilty of second-degree murder; guilty of tampering with evidence**, explicitly confirmed by the [Ninth Judicial Circuit State Attorney's official release](https://sao9.net/wp-content/uploads/6.17.22_-_ninth_judicial_circuit_state_attorneys_office_statement_-_danielle_redlick_verdict-1.pdf). The release webpage intermittently presents verification; the official PDF was readable.
- Sentence: August 5, 2022, 364 days in jail with credit for 364 days served, plus 12 months' probation and a mental-health evaluation/treatment condition, reported by [FOX 35 Orlando](https://www.fox35orlando.com/news/danielle-redlick-to-be-sentenced-for-tampering-with-evidence-following-death-of-husband-in-winter-park-home). [Court TV's sentencing recording page](https://www.courttv.com/title/kitchen-knife-murder-trial-danielle-redlick-sentenced/) independently describes the sentence.
- Latest official appellate action located: [May 30, 2023 opinion, case 6D23-1428](https://flcourts-media.flcourts.gov/content/download/2420878/opinion/Opinion_2023-1428.pdf), lower case **2019-CF-001846-A-O**, affirms without a written explanation. It does not establish current supervision or whether a later mandate issued. Obtain current clerk docket, judgment, sentencing order and mandate before a final script. Do not describe her as currently on probation.
- Her testimony that the stabbing was self-defense must be attributed to her; prosecution interpretations remain attributed arguments. [FOX 35's verdict reporting](https://www.fox35orlando.com/news/danielle-redlick-verdict-jurors-find-florida-woman-not-guilty-of-killing-husband) covers both sides and the acquittal.

## Proposed package and story

Two title options:

1. **She Admitted the Stabbing. The Jury Acquitted Her of Murder.**
2. **Danielle Redlick: The 911 Call, the Self-Defense Claim, and the Verdict**

Thumbnail concept: a licensed courtroom still of Danielle at the witness stand, with concise text **TWO VERDICTS** and a clearly readable **MURDER: ACQUITTED** line. The inspected 01:37 frame shows her in a gray blazer at the stand, but broadcaster graphics dominate it; obtain a suitable licensed frame/master. No invented weapon, blood, expression or crime-scene composite. A verdict document could support the layout after its authentic copy is obtained.

Suggested story progression, adaptable within 20–35 minutes:

1. A short juxtaposition of her admission and the two verdicts, making the acquittal clear early.
2. Michael's death and the emergency response; establish only what source records support.
3. The changing accounts and physical/digital evidence; distinguish testimony, exhibits and prosecution arguments.
4. Her self-defense account and relevant corroborating/contradicting testimony, given enough space to be intelligible.
5. The jury's separate decisions, then the evidence-tampering sentence and documented appellate result. Avoid inventing juror reasoning.
